幻灯片 04

Why Scale-Up Is NOT Scale-Out

00:29:04 — 00:31:57 · Josh Collier（预录视频） 官网回放 ↗
Josh Collier（预录视频）
00:29:04

For a long time, the Scale-Up network was treated as an implementation detail, whatever glue held a handful of accelerators together inside a box. That is no longer true.

00:29:15

In a modern AI rack, Scale-Up and Scale-Out are two different networks with two different optima. And Scale-Up is now the dominant network by almost any measure you pick.

00:29:27

Scale-Up demands up to 18 times more bandwidth than Scale-Out. AI workloads demand extremely low latency because Scale-Up sits inside the critical path of a single inference or training step.

00:29:42

That bandwidth shows up as ports, rails, cables, optics, and switches. So Scale-Up now dominates system networking cost. It is where the spend actually lives. Borrowing a general purpose network helps your schedule, not your efficiency.

00:30:01

Volume multiplies every inefficiency. Whatever you give up per port, per switch, you give up across the entire rack. Hyper optimization makes a big difference. Imagine saving 100 watts per accelerator due to less port switches and optics or switching to DWDM optics.

00:30:23

There are companies deploying millions of accelerators per year. This is big savings. We can maximize protocol efficiency. A memory semantic wire protocol carries far less overhead than a general purpose framing, and that efficiency turns directly into fewer rails, switches, and cables,

00:30:43

so less power and lower cost. We can minimize latency everywhere, not just on the wire. That includes collective offloads, synchronizations, These only work when the protocol is standardized.

00:30:57

We can scale bandwidth by adding ports and rails rather than chasing ever higher port rates, which is the cheaper, lower power path. Co-packaged short-haul DWDM optics can cut both latency and power at multi-rack reach.

00:31:15

Optimizations provide huge efficiencies at scale, and scale is exactly where we are at. So the traffic is different. The physics are different. And the economics are different.

00:31:29

Scale-Up is not Scale-Out. And it cannot be a repurposed version of it. It must be hyper-optimized for this job, especially for the scale that AI inference is deployed. Every watt matters.

00:31:41

This is exactly what UALink is built to be. Why not just use Ethernet everywhere? Reuse is a time to market answer, not an efficiency one, and we will see that next.

00:32:14

But they start from Ethernet and adapt it to Scale-Up, and that is the reuse trap from the last slide in product form. UALink was designed for Scale-Up from the beginning, and that difference shows up in every row of this table.

00:32:30

On a good day, Ethernet framing lands at around 75% wire efficiency for memory semantic protocol, while UALink's memory semantic framing is about 89% because of that overhead.

00:32:42

00:32:59

UALink is a latency-optimized path with low-latency switching. UALink already has multiple suppliers, which drives costs down. The alternatives are thinner, they carry premium pricing.

00:33:12

UALink specifies security and collective offloads in the standard rather than every vendor building custom protocol and controller IP. Open security standards are critical for building trust.

00:33:25

00:33:50

幻灯片 06

UALink Future Opportunities

00:34:19 — 00:37:09 · Josh Collier（预录视频） 官网回放 ↗
Josh Collier（预录视频）
00:34:19

I've made the case today for UALink. While UALink is already an impressive network technology, well suited for today's AI workloads, there are more opportunities. Bigger domains, a richer feature set, and lower latency power efficient optics are all interesting.

00:34:37

Today, UALink supports 1024 accelerators in a high bandwidth domain. It is clear that many large frontier LLMs can benefit from larger high bandwidth domains. Models and expert counts continue to climb.

00:34:53

A larger domain is what lets you run wide expert parallelism without splitting the model across fabrics. Bigger pods also amortize cost and power. Efficiency actually improves as the domain grows because memory is pooled across the whole pod instead of stranded per device.

00:35:12

And bigger pods potentially require multi-hop and or multi-dimensional topology so the domain can be tuned to the workload rather than forced onto one flat switch tier. Of course, the UALink specification continues to evolve.

00:35:28

Enhancing resiliency is critical. A bigger topology simply has more components in it, so the failure rate of the domain goes up. The fabric must detect, contain, and recover rather than take the whole pod down.

00:35:44

Congestion management is important. At this scale, incast and concurrent collectives are real traffic patterns, and that needs managing in the protocol, not worked around in software.

00:35:57

And of course, we are still finding how much collective and synchronization offload work the fabric can absorb on behalf of the accelerators.

00:36:08

Obviously, larger domains only pay off if each hop stays cheap in latency. Co-packaged DWDM optics will help us there. They promise both cleaner optical channels, which need less SerDes DSP, lighter equalization, and light FEC instead of heavy correction, so we save latency on every hop.

00:36:28

And critically, DWDM comes at power parity with today's electrical channels, but at multi-rack reach. So we get lower latency switching across those larger fabrics without paying an optics power penalty.

00:36:43

Put it all together, larger domains, a fabric hardened with resiliency, congestion control and offloads, and low latency optics at copper power. That's a fabric that keeps scaling with the models.

00:36:55

And it's only possible because Scale-Up is open, standardized, and memory semantic. These architectural directions are all significant potential opportunities for UALink

Heterogeneous PODs on One Fabric

Josh Collier（预录视频）
00:37:10

Let's briefly touch on heterogeneous systems, as we are likely going to see a significant shift in this direction. Today a pod is basically N copies of the same accelerator.

00:37:23

But a modern LLM model isn't uniform. Attention and expert FFN stress completely different resources. Attention is bound by KV cache bandwidth and latency. Expert FFN is dense matrix throughput and capacity.

00:37:39

Building one device to be great at both means compromising on each. Similar trade-offs are made for pre-fill and decode. There can be many different device types connected in a Scale-Up network: attention engines, expert FFN engines, memory and storage, and other potential emerging accelerators.

00:38:00

The key visual is that they all connect down into one UALink fabric. Because it's memory semantic, any device can address Pod memory directly and achieve the lowest communication latencies.

00:38:15

That is what makes mixing device types actually work rather than just co-locating them. The right silicon for each phase of the workload. We can size attention and FFN independently.

00:38:26

Memory can be pooled across device types, preventing capacity from being stranded behind one class of device. Independent scaling of different accelerators allows us to add expert capacity, change attention throughput, or storage as model shapes and serving mixes shift.

00:38:43

This is only possible because Scale-Up is an open, standardized memory semantic fabric, exactly the properties argued for earlier. A proprietary or Ethernet-derived stack can't offer this cleanly.

00:38:57
